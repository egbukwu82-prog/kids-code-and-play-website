From 30 kids to 10,000.

Since 2021, one volunteer engineer has taught every class and paid for the program himself. To grow beyond that, we're building a national program with paid instructors, cohorts in every province, and free classes for every family that needs them.

What we've built so far

Five years of proof, at no cost to families.

We started on Zoom during the pandemic for immigrant and low-income families in Calgary. The model works. What's missing is the funding to take it further.

80kids registered from 56 families

The plan

Four stages to a national program.

Each stage is triggered by funding, not a calendar. When one is funded, we open the next set of cohorts.

  1. 30 Now · Cohort 3

    Proven model

    One cohort taught by our founder with volunteer mentors. Ages 5–15, Sundays on Zoom.

  2. 500 Stage 1 · Alberta

    Paid instructors

    Hire and train instructors so multiple cohorts can run at once across Alberta.

  3. 1,000 Stage 2 · Across Canada

    Cohorts in every province

    Partner with schools and community groups to reach families nationwide, and launch Youth Entrepreneurship Futures, our AI program for ages 13–25.

  4. 10,000 Stage 3 · National program

    Runs on its own

    A staffed organization with steady funding, standing curriculum, and leadership beyond its founder.

Where the money goes

Every dollar goes to getting kids into class.

1

Paid instructors

The biggest need. Paying instructors lets us run many cohorts at once, so the program no longer depends on one person.

2

Laptops for kids

Python is hard to learn on a phone. Some of our students don't have a computer at home.

3

Curriculum & tools

Coding platforms, licences, and a curriculum new instructors can teach from on day one.

4

Running the program

Outreach, safety checks for instructors and volunteers, and the admin behind every cohort.
