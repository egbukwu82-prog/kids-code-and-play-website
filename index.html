How we teach

        
Think. Code. Build. Play.

        
Every class moves through the same four stages. Kids reason before they type, learn one real concept at a time, then use it with a team on something that works.

      

      

        

          
Think

          
Conundrums

          
A short story with no right answer. Kids pick a side and defend it out loud.

          reasoning · speaking
        

        

          
Code

          
One concept + 100 Days of Python

          
A short lesson on one idea, like functions, APIs or how YouTube works behind the scenes, then kids keep practising on Replit at home, at their own pace.

          replit.com · python
        

        

          
Build

          
Team projects

          
Every week, groups of 5–6 build games and real-life apps in breakout rooms, with a captain, a driver and navigators, and an adult mentor nearby.

          teamwork · leadership
        

        

          
Play

          
The play in our name

          
Every class ends with games, often ones classmates built. It's why kids come back next Sunday.

          community · fun
        

      

    

  


  

    

      

        
A KCAP Sunday

        
Two hours, every skill named.

        
Every third class, the concept slot becomes a system design or money basics session. 100 Days of Python happens at home, and we check each kid's progress on Sundays. We aim to keep every kid at about 80–85% success: hard enough to learn, not so hard they give up.

Conundrums

        
Every class starts with a question that has no right answer.

        
Before anyone writes code, kids practise thinking out loud: taking a position, listening to the other side, and changing their mind when the argument is better.

        
    
          
  • Builds the confidence to speak up in a group
    • 
          
  • Teaches that good ideas need good reasons
    • 
          
  • Often turns into the logic kids code that same day

The basics

        
Is it really free?
Yes. There are no fees, no materials to buy and no hidden costs. The program is funded by donations, grants and our founders.

        
Who can join?
Kids ages 5–15. Younger coders (5–9) join the first 30 minutes of class, the Conundrum and the new concept, and are free to leave after that or stay longer. We were founded for immigrant and low-income families and still prioritise them, but every family is welcome. We're starting in Calgary and Alberta and opening across Canada.

        
Does my child need coding experience?
No. Groups are organised by skill, so beginners start at the beginning and experienced kids get harder work.

        
Schedule and setup

        
When are classes?
Every Sunday, 4:00–6:00 PM Mountain Time, starting November 29, 2026.

        
What device does my child need?
A laptop or desktop computer (Windows or Mac) with a stable internet connection. Python is hard to learn on a phone or tablet. If you don't have a computer, tell us on the form. We're working on a device program.

        
Is there homework?
A little. Kids work through 100 Days of Python on Replit at home, at their own pace, and we check everyone's progress in class each Sunday.

        
Safety and privacy

        
How do you keep classes safe?
The Zoom link goes only to confirmed families and is never posted publicly. The waiting room is on, and we check names against our registration list before admitting anyone.

        
What do you do with my child's information?
We collect only what we need to run the class and contact you. It's stored privately, shared only with our team, and never sold or published.
